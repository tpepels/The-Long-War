# Reference decks

These six 40-card decks are balance/playtest reference decks built from the canonical 95-card pool. They are deliberately strategic builds, not coverage decks: cards are selected for the deck plan rather than to force every title into play.

## Strategic identities

### Mobility / Open Bonds

Built around sideways movement, incomplete/open-Bond formations, prepared cards, and chained position changes.

The new baseline expansion adds:

- **The Vardai** - an uncomplicated Force that can Maneuver before becoming Named.
- **The Ilyri** - rewards maintaining an open Bond.

### Persistent Elite / Heroes

Built around valuable Named Formations that survive across Battles.

The new baseline expansion adds:

- **A Hundred Shields** - a large vanilla body.
- **The Aradai** - becomes substantially stronger once Named.

This remains the most Hero-heavy deck with five Heroes.

### Narrative / Command

Built around generating, preserving, and converting Command through Narratives.

The new baseline expansion adds:

- **Thirty Spears** - cheap vanilla Force density.
- **The Aradai** - rewards the deck's desire to complete Named Formations.

It remains the most Narrative-heavy deck with seven Narratives.

### Battlefield Control / Stratagems

Built around Front geometry, Strength denial, Front-specific comparison rules, and Battle-wide plans.

The new baseline expansion adds:

- **The Damar** - an efficient Frontline-only body.
- **The Serekh** - resists Strength-denial effects.

It remains the most Stratagem-heavy deck with seven Stratagems.

### Momentum / Orders

Built around accepting constrained movement in exchange for tempo. **Had Been Ordered Forward**, **The King Had Given the Order**, and **The Line Had Begun to Move** reward committing to a direction instead of preserving perfect flexibility.

This is the cleaner test deck for self-imposed compulsion.

### Necessity / Attrition

Built around making contested Fronts difficult to abandon and forcing both players to keep committing resources. **No One Would Be First to Leave**, **There Was No Road Back**, **Every Banner Turned Toward Them**, and **They Had Gone Too Far** are the core necessity package.

This is the harsher test deck for mutual compulsion and overextension.

## Baseline Force naming rule

The Force pool now deliberately distinguishes between two naming styles.

### Generic stat bodies

Forces with no rules text may have plain military names:

- **Thirty Spears**
- **The Fifty Men**
- **A Hundred Shields**

Their plain titles signal that there is little or nothing to remember.

### Distinctive Forces

Once a Force has a mechanical property, prefer a memorable cultural, legendary, or otherwise distinctive name:

- **The Vardai**
- **The Aradai**
- **The Ilyri**
- **The Damar**
- **The Serekh**

The title carries flavour while the rules text can remain short.

## Cross-deck inclusion signal

Across the original four reference decks after adding the baseline Force expansion:

- no card appears in all four decks;
- only **The Wall Did Not Break** appears in three decks;
- the new Forces appear in one or two decks according to strategy;
- **The Fifty Men** appears in only one deck.

This strongly suggests that its former 4/4 inclusion was at least partly caused by a shortage of clean baseline Force alternatives rather than proving the card itself was inherently overpowered.

### The Wall Did Not Break - 3/4 decks

This remains the clearest broadly useful inclusion.

Its condition is forgiving because a tie satisfies "did not lose"; it can refund Command and, on a win, recover a Bond. Keep watching it in actual play.

## Omitted-card signal

### Covered the Withdrawal of - 0/4 decks

> After an adjacent formation you control Retreats, you may Maneuver this formation for 0 Command.

This remains the only canonical card omitted from all four decks, despite Mobility and Battlefield Control both being plausible homes.

Its effect requires an adjacent loss/Retreat, suitable positioning, and a useful Maneuver afterwards. It remains the clearest candidate for being too narrow.

## Interpretation

These are deck-building signals, not balance verdicts.

Current cards to watch:

Potentially too generically attractive:
- **The Wall Did Not Break**

Potentially too narrow:
- **Covered the Withdrawal of**

The newly added Forces should be evaluated primarily for whether they create real deck-building alternatives rather than becoming new automatic inclusions.


## Necessity / compulsion batch

Cards 88-95 are approved and are now concentrated in two dedicated reference decks rather than diluted across the original four:

- **Momentum / Orders** tests self-imposed direction, free forced movement, and momentum.
- **Necessity / Attrition** tests mutual commitment, Front fixation, and overextension.

Together the two decks include all eight Batch 12 cards. Their purpose is to expose whether compulsion creates interesting constrained decisions or merely removes too much agency.

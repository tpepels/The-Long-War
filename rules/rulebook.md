# The Long War

*Three Fronts. One hand. Spend carefully.*

<div class="rulebook-opening">

**The Long War** is a two-player card game fought across **Left**, **Center**, and **Right**. Each Battle ends when both players **Pass**. Control two Fronts to win the Battle; win two Battles to win the match.

Cards are the real cost. The battlefield is cleared after every Battle, but cards left in your hand stay with you. If you spend too much to win now, you may start the next Battle short of options.

On the battlefield, position matters. **Subjects** stand in the **Frontline** or **Rear**. Their roles reward different formations. **Bonds** and **Names** strengthen them. **Stories**, **Veiled Stories**, and **Stratagems** change the position without adding another Subject.

</div>

> **First game?** Read through **A Battle, from beginning to end**, then start playing. Use the Reference section when a question comes up.

### Reading cards and rules

**Bold** words are game terms or rules actions: **Subject**, **Bond**, **Name**, **Story**, **Stratagem**, **Strength**, **Front**, **Pass**, **discard**, **return**, and **move**.

*Italic* words are card titles or printed properties: *Namar*, *Swordsman*, *Human*, *Hero*, *Myth*, *Frontline only*.

<div class="rulebook-kicker">THE GAME IN BRIEF</div>

## Objective {#learn}

Every Battle is divided into three **Fronts**.

Each Front has two positions on each side:

- **Frontline**
- **Rear**

When both players have Passed, compare Strength in each Front.

- Higher Strength controls the Front.
- Equal Strength controls it for neither player.
- Control **2 of the 3 Fronts** to win the Battle.
- Win **2 Battles** to win the match.

A large lead in one Front is still only one Front. If your opponent spends heavily on Center, leaving Center alone and taking Left and Right may be the better play.

<div class="rulebook-big-rule">
  <span>3</span><small>Fronts in every Battle</small>
  <span>2</span><small>Fronts to win the Battle</small>
  <span>2</span><small>Battles to win the match</small>
</div>

### Your hand lasts longer than the battlefield

At the end of a Battle, cards on the battlefield are discarded. Cards still in your hand remain, then each player draws 3 cards.

So the question is not only whether you can win a Battle. It is how many cards you are willing to spend on it.

## What happens on a turn? {#turn}

On your turn:

1. You may set your one **Stratagem** for the Battle, if you have not already done so.
2. Then play **one card** or **Pass**.

Setting a Stratagem does not use your normal action.

There is no attack step and Subjects do not deal damage. You change the position by placing Subjects, attaching Bonds and Names, playing Stories, setting hidden cards, and moving or removing cards.

Once you **Pass**, you take no more turns in that Battle. Your opponent may continue until they also Pass.

<div class="rulebook-kicker">THE BATTLEFIELD</div>

## Frontline and Rear {#battlefield}

Each player has six Subject positions:

```text
                         OPPONENT

              LEFT        CENTER        RIGHT
              Rear         Rear          Rear
           Frontline    Frontline     Frontline
        =========================================
                       BATTLE LINE
        =========================================
           Frontline    Frontline     Frontline
              Rear         Rear          Rear
              LEFT        CENTER        RIGHT

                           YOU
```

Only Subjects occupy these spaces. A Bond and Name attach to a Subject and stay with that Subject unless a rule removes them.

### Line Defense

Every Subject in the **Frontline** gets **+1 Strength**.

That bonus is called **Line Defense**. It applies automatically unless a card says otherwise.

## Subject roles {#roles}

Every Subject has a printed *role*. Roles have standard formation rules.

| Role | Rule |
| --- | --- |
| *Swordsman* | +1 Strength in the **Frontline**. |
| *Spearman* | +1 Strength in the **Frontline** if a friendly Subject is directly behind it. |
| *Archer* | +2 Strength in the **Rear** if a friendly Subject is directly in front of it. |
| *Healer* | While in the **Rear**, the friendly Subject directly in front of it gets +2 Strength. Current Healer cards are printed *Rear only*. |
| *Ship* | +1 Strength in the **Rear**. |
| *Stronghold* | +1 Strength in the **Rear**. |

Role bonuses are added after Line Defense.

A Swordsman normally wants the Frontline. An Archer wants a Subject in front of it. A Spearman wants support behind it. A Healer contributes through the Subject ahead of it.

### Adjacent, in front of, behind

**Adjacent** means immediately left or right in the same rank on your side.

**In front of** means the Frontline position in the same Front.

**Behind** means the Rear position in the same Front.

If a Subject moves, recalculate any positional bonuses immediately.

## Classifications {#classifications}

Cards also have printed *classifications*, such as *Human*, *God*, *King*, *Ship*, *Fleet*, *Warband*, or *Stronghold*.

A classification has no rule by itself. It matters only when a card refers to it.

A card may have several classifications. A Subject's classification says what it is; its role says how it behaves in formation.

## Heroes {#heroes}

Every legal deck contains **exactly one Hero**.

A Hero is a unique Subject with a special rule. It uses an ordinary Subject position and follows the same placement and targeting rules unless its card says otherwise.

The reference deck uses *Avaros, the Bronze King*:

- printed Strength 6;
- *Swordsman*;
- *Human · King · Hero*;
- while Avaros is in the Frontline, adjacent Subjects you control get +1 Strength.

In the Frontline, Avaros counts 8 before his adjacency rule: 6 printed +1 Line Defense +1 Swordsman.

<div class="rulebook-kicker">BUILDING A POSITION</div>

## Subject — Bond — Name {#cards}

A Subject, Bond, and Name can form a sentence:

<div class="legend-sentence">
  <span class="subject"><em>The Fifty Men</em></span><i>—</i>
  <span class="link"><em>Followed</em></span><i>—</i>
  <span class="name"><em>Namar</em></span>
</div>

Read it as: *The Fifty Men followed Namar.*

They remain three separate cards.

### Subject

A **Subject** occupies a battlefield position.

Its Strength comes from its printed value plus Line Defense, role bonuses, attached cards, and other modifiers.

### Bond

A **Bond** attaches to one of your Subjects.

A Bond applies its printed effect immediately. Many Bonds gain an additional effect once a Name is attached.

A Bond without a Name is an **open Bond**.

### Name

A **Name** attaches to an open Bond.

The Name adds its printed Strength and rules. Any Bond rule that requires a Name also becomes active.

Every Name is *Unique*.

## Stories {#stories}

A **Story** changes the board without occupying a Subject position.

Every Story has a printed *form*:

- *Myth*
- *Legend*
- *Saga*
- *Omen*
- *Warning*
- *Prophecy*
- *Conspiracy*

The form has no general rule. It matters only if another card refers to it.

### Immediate Stories

An immediate Story is played face-up, resolved in full, then discarded.

Examples:

- *The Story Is False* — *Myth*
- *He Never Came* — *Legend*
- *They Chose Another* — *Saga*

### Veiled Stories

A Story with the *Veiled* property is played face-down in a Front. You may have at most one Veiled Story in each Front.

A face-down Veiled Story contributes the hidden Front bonus printed in its rule. Most current Veiled Stories give +1 Strength; *No Fires Burned* gives +2.

When the printed trigger occurs:

1. reveal the Story;
2. remove its face-down Front bonus;
3. resolve its effect;
4. discard it.

The opponent can see which Front contains a Veiled Story, but not which Story it is.

The reference set contains:

- *The Lamps Went Dark* — *Omen*
- *The Road Was Cut* — *Warning*
- *The Hidden Oars* — *Prophecy*
- *The Witness Lied* — *Conspiracy*

## Stratagems {#stratagems}

A **Stratagem** is a hidden Battle-wide card. It does not belong to a Front.

On your own turn, before your normal action, you may set one Stratagem from your hand face-down. Each player may do this **once per Battle**.

Setting it is free: you still play one card or Pass afterward.

Once set, the Stratagem stays in play until the Battle ends. Revealing it does not let you set another.

When its printed trigger occurs:

1. reveal it;
2. resolve any one-time reveal effect;
3. leave it face-up;
4. apply its continuing rule for the rest of the Battle.

If it never triggers, reveal it only after the Battle has been scored, then discard it. A Stratagem that stayed hidden cannot change an already determined result.

Both players may have a Stratagem in play. Strength modifiers from revealed Stratagems stack. A rule that switches something off, such as *The Tide Rose* disabling Line Defense, remains off while that Stratagem is revealed.

The reference deck uses six deliberately double-edged Stratagems:

- *The Storm Broke* helps Ships and hinders Archers.
- *The Tide Rose* helps Ships and removes Line Defense.
- *The Ground Gave Way* helps Rear Subjects and weakens Frontline Subjects.
- *The Bronze Teeth* punishes an opposing Frontline arrival, then weakens your own Frontline.
- *The False Muster* cancels one opposing immediate Story, then prevents you from playing immediate Stories.
- *The Wooden Gift* weakens named Subjects and strengthens unnamed Subjects.

The expanded pool also contains *The Broken Mast* and *The Quiet Field*. Their full text is authoritative; they follow the same set/reveal/continue timing above.

## Building one Frontline Subject {#three-card-line}

You play *The Fifty Men* in Center Frontline.

| Step | Calculation | Strength |
| --- | --- | ---: |
| Printed Strength | *The Fifty Men* | 4 |
| Line Defense | Frontline | +1 |
| *Swordsman* | Frontline role bonus | +1 |
| **Current total** | | **6** |

You attach *Followed*:

| Addition | Strength |
| --- | ---: |
| Existing Subject | 6 |
| *Followed* | +1 |
| **Current total** | **7** |

You then attach *Namar*:

- *Namar*: +2;
- *Followed* now has a Name: +2;
- *Namar* is in the Frontline: +2.

The position is now **13 Strength**.

That took three cards and three turns, all in Center. If the opponent gives up Center and wins Left and Right, the 13 Strength does not save the Battle.

<div class="rulebook-kicker">A BATTLE, FROM BEGINNING TO END</div>

## Follow one Battle {#walkthrough}

### Turn 1 — Player 1 takes Center

Player 1 plays *The Fifty Men* in Center Frontline.

They count as 6 Strength: 4 printed +1 Line Defense +1 for being Swordsmen in the Frontline.

### Turn 2 — Player 2 uses the Rear

Player 2 plays *Seven Black Ships* in Right Rear.

Ships get +1 in the Rear, so the position is 5 Strength.

### Turn 3 — Player 1 adds depth

Player 1 plays *The Children of the Salt Road* in Center Rear.

They are Archers. *The Fifty Men* are directly in front of them, so the Archers get +2.

### Turn 4 — Player 2 sets a Veiled Story

Player 2 plays *The Lamps Went Dark* face-down in Center.

It gives +1 Strength to Center while face-down. Player 1 knows a Veiled Story is there, but not its identity.

### Turn 5 — Player 1 adds a Bond

Player 1 attaches *Followed* to *The Fifty Men*.

The Bond adds Strength immediately. A Name can be attached later.

### Turn 6 — Player 2 changes Fronts

Instead of spending more cards on Center, Player 2 plays a Subject in Left.

Center may already be expensive to contest. Left and Right can still win the Battle.

### Later — Player 1 Passes

Suppose Player 1 controls Center and narrowly controls Left. Player 1 Passes.

Player 2 may keep playing. They now decide whether taking Left is worth the cards it will cost.

If Player 2 also Passes, score the Battle.

## Passing {#passing}

When you **Pass**, you take no more turns in that Battle.

The opponent may continue playing one card per turn until they Pass.

Passing saves the rest of your hand, but gives the opponent as many turns as they are willing to pay for.

<div class="rulebook-kicker">START PLAYING</div>

## Set up your first match {#setup}

1. Each player takes a legal **30-card deck** with **exactly one Hero**.
2. Shuffle and draw **10 cards**.
3. Each player may choose up to **2 cards**, shuffle them back, then draw the same number.
4. Randomly choose the first player for Battle I.
5. Keep hands and decks hidden. Keep discard piles face-up.

There is no automatic draw at the start of a turn.

## Score the Battle {#scoring}

When both players have Passed:

1. calculate each occupied Subject's Strength;
2. add Frontline and Rear within each Front;
3. apply any Front-level modifiers;
4. compare the three Fronts.

A player controls a Front only if their Strength there is higher.

Determine the Battle winner in this order:

1. the player who controls **2 of 3 Fronts**;
2. if neither player controls two, the player with higher **total Strength** across all three Fronts;
3. if total Strength is tied, the player who **Passed first**.

## After the Battle {#after-battle}

The winner takes 1 Victory marker.

Then:

1. reveal any Stratagem still face-down; it cannot change the score;
2. discard all battlefield cards, including Veiled Stories and Stratagems;
3. keep cards still in hand;
4. each player draws 3 cards;
5. the Battle loser chooses who takes the first turn of the next Battle.

The first player to win 2 Battles wins the match.

## Strength {#reference}

For each occupied Subject position:

1. start with printed Strength;
2. add Line Defense if it is in the Frontline;
3. apply its role;
4. apply support from Subjects behind, in front of, or adjacent;
5. apply the attached Bond;
6. if a Name is attached, add its printed Strength and any Bond or Name conditions;
7. apply temporary and card-specific modifiers.

A Front's Strength is its Frontline + Rear + Front-level modifiers.

Final Subject Strength cannot fall below 0.

## Position language

**Front**  
One complete Left, Center, or Right column.

**Frontline**  
The Subject position nearest the battle line. It normally receives Line Defense.

**Rear**  
The Subject position behind the Frontline.

**Adjacent**  
Immediately left or right in the same rank on the same player's side.

**Behind**  
The Rear position in the same Front.

**In front of**  
The Frontline position in the same Front.

**Opposing**  
On the opponent's side of the same Front.

**Open Bond**  
A Bond attached to a Subject without a Name.

A Bond and Name are always in the same Front and rank as their Subject.

## Playing cards precisely

### Subjects

Choose a legal empty Subject position.

Unless the card says otherwise, a Subject may be played in the Frontline or Rear. Positional bonuses update whenever it moves.

### Bonds

Choose one of your Subjects without a Bond and attach the Bond.

A Subject can have at most one Bond.

### Names

Choose one of your open Bonds and attach the Name.

A Bond can have at most one Name. Every Name is Unique.

### Stories

For an immediate Story, choose all required legal targets, resolve the text in order, then discard the Story.

For a Veiled Story, choose a Front with no Veiled Story on your side and play it face-down.

You may inspect your own face-down Stories. Your opponent may not.

### Stratagems

Before your normal action, if you have not set a Stratagem this Battle, you may set one from your hand face-down in your Stratagem space.

Do not end the turn. You must still play one card or Pass.

A revealed Stratagem stays face-up until the Battle ends. You cannot set another Stratagem that Battle.

## What happens when attached cards leave?

During a Battle, when an effect removes or moves an attached card:

| What happens? | Result |
| --- | --- |
| A **Subject** is discarded by an effect | Discard its Bond. Return its Name to its owner's hand. |
| A **Bond** is discarded by an effect | The Subject stays. Return its Name to its owner's hand. |
| An open **Bond** is returned | The Subject stays. Put the Bond into its owner's hand. |
| A **Name** is returned or discarded | Subject and Bond stay. The Bond becomes open. |
| A **Subject** moves | Its Bond and Name move with it. |

**Battle cleanup is different:** after scoring, every card still on the battlefield is discarded together, including attached Bonds and Names.

## Timing

There is no reaction stack. A Stratagem is set only on its controller's turn, before that player's normal action.

For a normal card play:

1. announce the card and all required targets;
2. if it is an immediate Story and an opposing *The False Muster* triggers, reveal the Stratagem and cancel that Story;
3. otherwise resolve the played card completely;
4. resolve any Veiled Stories triggered by that play;
5. reveal and resolve any Stratagems triggered by that play.

*The False Muster* cancels an immediate Story before that Story resolves. It never cancels a Veiled Story.

When a player Passes:

1. mark that player as Passed;
2. resolve Pass-triggered Stratagems;
3. resolve Pass-triggered Veiled Stories;
4. if both players have Passed, score the Battle.

If both hidden Stratagems trigger from the same event, reveal the active player's Stratagem first, then the opponent's. Apply all continuing effects together afterward.

Reveal any Stratagem still face-down only after scoring. Its newly revealed continuing effect cannot change the result that was already determined.

## Hidden and public information

**Hidden**

- cards in hand;
- the identity of an unrevealed Veiled Story;
- the identity of an unrevealed Stratagem.

**Public**

- hand size;
- deck size;
- discard piles;
- all face-up battlefield cards;
- which Front contains each face-down Veiled Story;
- whether each player has a face-down Stratagem;
- whether each player has used their Stratagem opportunity this Battle;
- classifications and roles on visible cards.

## Deck construction

A deck contains exactly **30 cards**.

- Exactly **1 Hero**.
- Maximum 2 copies of a non-Unique title.
- Maximum 1 copy of a Unique title.
- Every Name is Unique.
- Subjects, Bonds, Names, Stories, and Stratagems all use the same deck.

<div class="rulebook-kicker">GLOSSARY</div>

## Glossary {#glossary}

**Battle**  
One scoring period. It ends when both players have Passed.

**Bond**  
A card attached to a Subject. A Bond without a Name is an open Bond.

**Classification**  
A printed identity property such as *Human*, *God*, *King*, *Ship*, or *Stronghold*.

**Hero**  
A unique Subject with a special rule. Every deck contains exactly one Hero.

**Line Defense**  
The +1 Strength normally gained by a Subject in the Frontline.

**Name**  
A Unique card attached to a Bond.

**Pass**  
End your participation in the current Battle.

**Role**  
A printed Subject property such as *Swordsman*, *Spearman*, *Archer*, or *Healer*. Roles provide standard formation rules.

**Story**  
A one-shot card. Its form may be *Myth*, *Legend*, *Saga*, *Omen*, *Warning*, *Prophecy*, or *Conspiracy*.

**Stratagem**  
A Battle-wide hidden card set before your normal action. Each player may set one per Battle.

**Subject**  
The only card type that occupies a battlefield position.

**Veiled Story**  
A Story with the *Veiled* property, played face-down in a Front until its trigger reveals it.

**Strength**  
The value used to determine control of Fronts.

*Unique*  
A printed property limiting that title to one copy per deck.

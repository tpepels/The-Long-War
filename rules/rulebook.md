# The Long War

*Four Fronts. One hand. One long war.*

<div class="rulebook-opening" markdown="1">

**The Long War** is a two-player card game fought across **four Fronts**. Players build Forces into lasting formations, shift them across the line, spend Command to keep pressure on the enemy, and decide when to stop committing to the current Battle.

Every operation costs time. Most cards also cost **Command**, tracked on a d20 from 20 down to 0. Command persists between Battles. The longer the war continues, the less Command returns.

A completed **Force — Bond — Name** is a **Named Formation**. Named Formations can survive from one Battle into the next. Lose their Front and they are pushed back: Frontline formations retreat to the Rear; Rear formations are driven off.

There is no overall winner of a Battle. Each Front matters on its own. Losing Fronts costs position and reduces the Command you recover. The war ends when one side can no longer sustain enough Command to keep pace.

</div>

> **First game?** Read through **A Battle, from beginning to end**, then start playing. Use the Reference section when a question comes up.

### Reading cards and rules

**Bold** words are game terms or rules actions: **Force**, **Bond**, **Name**, **Named Formation**, **Story**, **Stratagem**, **Strength**, **Front**, **Command**, **Maneuver**, **Retreat**, **Pass**, **discard**, **return**, **draw**, and **move**.

*Italic* words are card titles or printed properties: *Namar*, *Swordsman*, *Human*, *Hero*, *Myth*, *Unique*, *Deploy — Frontline only*.

<div class="rulebook-kicker">THE GAME IN BRIEF</div>

## Objective {#learn}

The battlefield is divided into **four Fronts**.

Each Front has two positions on each side:

- **Frontline**
- **Rear**

After the first Pass and the opponent's final operation, compare Strength in each Front separately.

- Higher Strength wins that Front.
- Equal Strength means the Front is tied.
- A tied Front causes no Retreat and no attrition.
- Losing a Front pushes persistent formations backward and reduces the Command you recover.

The war continues from Battle to Battle until **Command Collapse** ends it. Battle victories are not counted separately.

<div class="rulebook-big-rule">
  <span>4</span><small>Fronts in every Battle</small>
  <span>20</span><small>maximum Command</small>
  <span>5</span><small>Command Collapse threshold</small>
</div>

### The battlefield lasts longer than a Battle

Cards in hand, the draw pile, the discard pile, Command, ongoing Stories, and **Named Formations** can all carry forward.

A completed Named Formation that survives a Battle stays on the battlefield.

An incomplete formation does not. After Fronts are resolved, incomplete formations are cleared before persistent formations Retreat.

This means the board in the next Battle is shaped by what happened before it.

### Command is the war clock

Each player begins the war at **20 Command**, tracked on a d20.

Command spent during a Battle is not restored automatically. After each Battle, you recover some Command, but:

1. the base amount gets smaller as the war continues;
2. every Front you lost reduces that recovery by 1;
3. Command can never exceed 20.

The current playtest recovery schedule is:

| After Battle | Base recovery |
| --- | ---: |
| I | 10 |
| II | 7 |
| III | 5 |
| IV | 4 |
| V | 3 |
| VI | 2 |
| VII | 1 |
| VIII and later | 0 |

Your actual recovery is:

**base recovery − Fronts lost**, to a minimum of 0.

If you end a Battle on 6 Command, lose 3 Fronts after Battle I, and recover 10 − 3 = 7, you begin the next Battle on 13 Command.

### Command Collapse

After Command recovery:

- if exactly one player has fewer than **5 Command**, that player loses the war;
- if both players have fewer than 5 and one has less Command than the other, the lower player loses the war;
- if both players have fewer than 5 and are tied, the war continues.

The threshold of 5 and the recovery schedule are current playtest values.

## What happens on a turn? {#turn}

At the **start of every turn**, draw 1 card automatically.

Your hand may never contain more than **10 cards**. If you would draw while already holding 10, first discard 1 card from your hand, then draw.

Then take exactly **one operation**:

1. **Play one card** from your hand and pay its printed Command cost.
2. **Maneuver one Named Formation** for 1 Command.
3. **Pass** for 0 Command, once Pass is legal.

A card can change printed costs. Cost reductions cannot reduce a printed card below 1 Command unless a card explicitly says otherwise.

There is **no generic Draw operation**, no standard Cycle operation, and no generic Advance operation.

A **Stratagem** is played face-up as your operation for the turn, just like another card. Each player may play at most one new Stratagem per Battle.

There is no attack step and Forces do not deal damage. The position changes through deployment, formation building, Maneuver, Stories, Stratagems, card effects, and Retreat at Battle end.

Normally, the first Pass is not legal until both players have completed at least one operation. After the first player **Passes**, that player takes no more operations this Battle. The opponent receives exactly **one final operation**. The Battle then ends.

The player who Passed first starts the next Battle.

<div class="rulebook-kicker">THE BATTLEFIELD</div>

## Frontline and Rear {#battlefield}

Each player has eight Force positions:

                         OPPONENT

              FRONT 1   FRONT 2   FRONT 3   FRONT 4
                Rear      Rear      Rear      Rear
             Frontline Frontline Frontline Frontline
        =================================================
                       BATTLE LINE
        =================================================
             Frontline Frontline Frontline Frontline
                Rear      Rear      Rear      Rear
              FRONT 1   FRONT 2   FRONT 3   FRONT 4

                           YOU

Only Forces occupy these main positions. A Bond and Name attach to a Force's formation and move with it unless a rule says otherwise.

### Line Defense

Every Force in the **Frontline** gets **+1 Strength**.

That bonus is called **Line Defense**. It applies automatically unless a card says otherwise.

## Force roles {#roles}

Every Force has a printed *role*. Roles have standard formation rules.

| Role | Rule |
| --- | --- |
| *Swordsman* | +1 Strength in the **Frontline**. |
| *Spearman* | +1 Strength in the **Frontline** if a friendly Force is directly behind it. |
| *Archer* | +2 Strength in the **Rear** if a friendly Force is directly in front of it. |
| *Healer* | While in the **Rear**, the friendly Force directly in front of it gets +2 Strength. Current Healer cards are printed *Deploy — Rear only*. |
| *Ship* | +1 Strength in the **Rear**. |
| *Stronghold* | +1 Strength in the **Rear**. |

Role bonuses are added after Line Defense.

A Swordsman normally wants the Frontline. An Archer wants a Force in front of it. A Spearman wants support behind it. A Healer contributes through the Force ahead of it.

### Adjacent, in front of, behind

**Adjacent** means immediately left or right in the same rank on your side.

**In front of** means the Frontline position in the same Front.

**Behind** means the Rear position in the same Front.

If a Force moves, recalculate positional bonuses immediately.

## Classifications {#classifications}

Cards also have printed *classifications*, such as *Human*, *God*, *King*, *Ship*, *Fleet*, *Warband*, or *Stronghold*.

A classification has no rule by itself. It matters only when a card refers to it.

A card may have several classifications. A Force's classification says what it is; its role says how it behaves in formation.

## Deployment restrictions

A printed restriction such as:

**Deploy — Frontline only**

or:

**Deploy — Rear only**

restricts only where that Force may enter the battlefield.

It does not prevent later movement caused by Retreat or a card effect.

A Force deployed to the Frontline can therefore be pushed into the Rear. If a card's ability works only in a particular rank, the card says so.

## Heroes {#heroes}

Heroes are **Unique** cards. Each individual Hero title may appear only once in a deck.

Every Hero is dual-use: play it either as a **Force** or as a **Name**. Its card shows the rules that apply in each use.

You may play only **one new Hero per side per Battle**. Playing a Hero as either a Force or a Name uses that Battle's Hero allowance.

Heroes already present in persistent Named Formations remain on the battlefield and do not consume the next Battle's allowance.

A long war can therefore contain several Heroes who entered during different Battles.

<div class="rulebook-kicker">BUILDING A POSITION</div>

## Force — Bond — Name {#cards}

A Force, Bond, and Name can form a sentence:

<div class="legend-sentence">
  <span class="subject"><em>The Fifty Men</em></span><i>—</i>
  <span class="link"><em>Followed</em></span><i>—</i>
  <span class="name"><em>Namar</em></span>
</div>

Read it as: *The Fifty Men followed Namar.*

They remain three separate cards, and the three formation components may be played in **any order**.

A Bond or Name played before a Force is **prepared**. It stays face-up in that battlefield position, but contributes no Strength and no rule that depends on its Force until a Force is present.

### Force

A **Force** activates a battlefield formation.

Play it into any Frontline or Rear position that does not already contain a Force, subject to any printed deployment restriction. A prepared Bond or Name may already be there.

Its Strength comes from its printed value plus Line Defense, role bonuses, its Bond and/or Name, and other modifiers.

### Bond

A **Bond** occupies the Bond slot of one of your battlefield positions. It may be played before or after the Force and Name.

If no Force is present, the Bond is prepared. Once a Force is present, the Bond's Force-dependent rules become active.

A Bond without a Name is an **open Bond**.

### Name

A **Name** occupies the Name slot of one of your battlefield positions. It may be played before or after the Force and Bond.

If no Force is present, the Name is prepared. Once a Force is present, the Name adds its printed Strength and applicable rules even if the Bond slot is still empty.

Every printed Name is **Unique**.

### Named Formation

A formation containing a **Force, Bond, and Name** is a **Named Formation**.

Named Formations are persistent. If they remain on the battlefield at the end of a Battle, they carry forward into the next Battle after Retreat is resolved.

A Force with only a Bond, only a Name, or neither is not a Named Formation and does not persist by default.

This makes completing the sentence a major commitment: those cards can remain part of the war rather than being rebuilt from scratch.

## Maneuver {#maneuver}

A Named Formation may **Maneuver** as your operation.

Maneuver costs **1 Command**.

To Maneuver:

- choose one of your Named Formations;
- move it exactly one adjacent Front left or right;
- keep it in the same rank.

If the destination position is empty, move the Named Formation there.

If the destination contains another friendly formation, the two formations **exchange positions**. Only the formation initiating the Maneuver must be Named.

When formations exchange positions, move the complete contents of both positions: Force, Bond, Name, and any prepared components.

Maneuver never moves a formation from Rear to Frontline or Frontline to Rear.

There is no limit on how many times the same Named Formation may Maneuver during a Battle, provided you keep spending operations and Command.

There is no generic Maneuver between Battles.

## Stories {#stories}

A **Story** is played face-up.

Every Story has a printed *form*, such as:

- *Myth*
- *Legend*
- *Saga*
- *Omen*
- *Warning*
- *Prophecy*
- *Conspiracy*

A Story's form has no general rule by itself. It matters when card text refers to it.

Stories can work on different timescales.

An **immediate Story** resolves in full and is then discarded.

An **ongoing Story** remains face-up until its own text says that it completes, fails, resolves, expires, or is discarded.

Each player may have at most **3 ongoing Stories** at a time.

Immediate Stories do not occupy an ongoing Story slot.

When an ongoing Story ends, discard it and its slot becomes available again.

Stories normally belong to the player who played them. Their effects and objectives can still involve the opponent, a Front, a formation, or the wider war.

Stories are always public while they are in play.

## Stratagems {#stratagems}

A **Stratagem** is a public Battle-wide card. It does not belong to a Front.

On your own turn, you may use your operation to play one Stratagem from your hand **face-up**. Each player may play at most one new Stratagem per Battle.

Pay the Stratagem's printed Command cost. Playing it uses your operation for the turn.

The Stratagem is active immediately and normally remains face-up until the Battle ends.

Both players may have a Stratagem in play. Strength modifiers from active Stratagems stack. A rule that switches something off remains off while that Stratagem is active.

Stratagems describe **how this Battle is being fought**. Stories describe events, objectives, or developments that may be immediate or may outlast a Battle.

<div class="rulebook-kicker">A BATTLE, FROM BEGINNING TO END</div>

## Follow one Battle {#walkthrough}

### Turn 1 — Player 1 establishes a Front

Player 1 plays *The Fifty Men* in Front 2 Frontline.

They count as 6 Strength: 4 printed +1 Line Defense +1 for being Swordsmen in the Frontline.

### Turn 2 — Player 2 uses the Rear

Player 2 plays *Seven Black Ships* in Front 4 Rear.

Ships get +1 in the Rear, so the position is 5 Strength.

### Turn 3 — Player 1 adds depth

Player 1 plays *The Children of the Salt Road* in Front 2 Rear.

They are Archers. *The Fifty Men* are directly in front of them, so the Archers get their Rear support bonus.

### Turn 4 — Player 2 starts a Story

Player 2 plays an ongoing Story face-up.

If the Story remains unresolved at Battle end, it can continue into later Battles according to its text.

### Turn 5 — Player 1 completes a Named Formation

Player 1 attaches *Followed* and later *Namar* to *The Fifty Men*.

Once Force, Bond, and Name are all present, that position is a Named Formation and can persist.

### Later — Player 1 Maneuvers

Player 1 spends 1 Command and uses an operation to Maneuver that Named Formation from Front 2 to adjacent Front 1.

If Front 1 Frontline is empty, the formation moves there.

If Front 1 Frontline contains another friendly formation, the two formations exchange positions.

### Later — Player 2 Passes

Player 2 decides that further commitment is not worth the Command.

Player 2 Passes.

Player 1 receives exactly one final operation.

Then the Battle ends and all four Fronts are resolved separately.

## Passing {#passing}

Normally you may not **Pass** until both players have completed at least one operation. If no other legal action exists, Pass remains available.

When the first player Passes:

1. that player takes no more operations this Battle;
2. the opponent receives exactly **one final operation**;
3. after that operation — or if the opponent Passes — the Battle ends.

Passing preserves your remaining hand and Command, but gives the opponent one last chance to alter the four Front results.

The player who Passed first starts the next Battle.

<div class="rulebook-kicker">START PLAYING</div>

## Set up your first war {#setup}

1. Each player takes a **34-card playtest deck** with **14 Force-type cards** and **6 Name cards**. Hero cards count among the Force-type cards and may also be played as Names.
2. Each player places a d20 at **20 Command**.
3. Shuffle and draw **10 cards**.
4. Each player may choose up to **2 cards**, shuffle them back, then draw the same number.
5. Randomly choose the first player for Battle I.
6. The first player's turn begins with the normal automatic draw of 1 card.
7. Keep hands and decks hidden. Keep discard piles face-up.

Your hand limit is 10.

Whenever you would draw while holding 10 cards, first discard one card from your hand, then draw.

## Resolve the Fronts {#scoring}

After the first Pass and the opponent's final operation, resolve all four Fronts.

For each Front:

1. calculate the total Strength of each player's Frontline and Rear there;
2. apply any Front-level modifiers;
3. compare the two totals.

Equal Strength cancels completely: the Front is tied and nothing happens there.

If one player has more Strength, that player wins the Front and the other player loses it.

The size of the Strength margin has no additional core effect. It matters only if a card refers to it.

There is no overall Battle winner.

A Battle can therefore end 4–0, 3–1, 2–2, 1–1 with two tied Fronts, or in any other combination of local results.

## Retreat {#retreat}

After all four Front results are known, persistent formations in lost Fronts are pushed back.

First clear incomplete formations as described under **After the Battle**. They contributed to the Front result, but they do not persist and do not block Retreat.

Then, in each Front a player lost:

1. if that player has a Named Formation in the **Rear**, it is **driven off** and discarded;
2. if that player has a Named Formation in the **Frontline**, it **Retreats** to the now-empty Rear.

If only a Frontline Named Formation is present, it simply falls back to Rear.

If only a Rear Named Formation is present, it is driven off.

If the Front was tied, nothing Retreats.

Winning a Front does not move your formations. They hold their positions.

## After the Battle {#after-battle}

Use this order.

### 1. Record the four Front results

Mark each Front as won, lost, or tied for each player.

No overall Battle winner is determined.

### 2. Clear incomplete formations

Discard every battlefield formation that is not a complete Force — Bond — Name.

Prepared Bonds and Names are also discarded.

These cards contributed normally while the Battle was being fought, but only Named Formations persist.

### 3. Resolve Retreat

In each lost Front:

- drive off the Rear Named Formation, if any;
- move the Frontline Named Formation to Rear, if any.

Tied Fronts do nothing.

### 4. Clear Battle-only state

- discard Stratagems;
- end temporary Strength modifiers and other Battle-only effects;
- resolve or discard Stories only when their own text says so;
- leave unresolved ongoing Stories face-up.

### 5. Recover Command

Count how many Fronts you lost.

Take the base recovery for the Battle number and subtract 1 for each Front lost, to a minimum recovery of 0.

Then increase your Command by that amount, to a maximum of 20.

Example after Battle II:

- current Command: 5;
- base recovery: 7;
- Fronts lost: 2;
- actual recovery: 5;
- new Command: 10.

### 6. Check Command Collapse

After both players recover:

- if only one player is below 5 Command, that player loses the war;
- if both are below 5 and unequal, the player with less Command loses the war;
- if both are below 5 and tied, the war continues.

If the war ends, stop here.

### 7. Refill hands

Keep the cards still in your hand.

Keep the remaining draw pile and discard pile separate. Do **not** reshuffle merely because the Battle ended.

Draw until you have 10 cards.

If the deck empties while drawing, shuffle the discard pile to form a new deck and continue.

### 8. Reset Battle allowances

Each player may again play:

- one new Hero during the next Battle;
- one new Stratagem during the next Battle.

Persistent Heroes and ongoing Stories remain where they are.

### 9. Begin the next Battle

The battlefield stays as Retreat left it.

There is no between-Battle Maneuver.

The player who Passed first in the previous Battle starts.

## Strength {#reference}

For each position containing a Force:

1. start with the Force's printed Strength;
2. add Line Defense if it is in the Frontline;
3. apply its role;
4. apply support from Forces behind, in front of, or adjacent;
5. if a Bond is present, apply its active rules;
6. if a Name is present, add its printed Strength and active rules;
7. apply temporary and card-specific modifiers.

A position containing only a prepared Bond and/or Name contributes **0 Strength** until a Force is played there.

A Front's Strength is its Frontline + Rear + Front-level modifiers.

Final Force Strength cannot fall below 0.

## Position language

**Front**  
One complete column of the battlefield. There are four Fronts.

**Frontline**  
The Force position nearest the battle line. It normally receives Line Defense.

**Rear**  
The Force position behind the Frontline.

**Adjacent**  
Immediately left or right in the same rank on the same player's side.

**Behind**  
The Rear position in the same Front.

**In front of**  
The Frontline position in the same Front.

**Opposing**  
On the opponent's side of the same Front.

**Open Bond**  
A Bond without a Name, whether or not its formation already has a Force.

**Prepared**  
A Bond or Name already placed in a battlefield position that does not yet contain a Force. Force-dependent text is inactive until the Force arrives.

**Named Formation**  
A complete Force, Bond, and Name in one battlefield position. Named Formations persist between Battles by default.

All components in one formation share the same Front and rank.

## Playing cards precisely

### Forces

Choose one of your positions without a Force. A prepared Bond, Name, or both may already be in that position.

Unless the card says otherwise, a Force may be deployed in the Frontline or Rear.

A printed **Deploy** restriction applies only when the Force enters play.

Positional bonuses update whenever the Force moves.

### Bonds

Choose one of your positions without a Bond and play the Bond there.

A Force or Name may already be present, or the Bond may be prepared in an otherwise incomplete formation.

A formation can have at most one Bond.

### Names

Choose one of your positions without a Name and play the Name there.

A Force or Bond may already be present, or the Name may be prepared in an otherwise incomplete formation.

A formation can have at most one Name.

Every printed Name is **Unique**.

### Stories

Immediate Stories are played face-up, resolved in order, then discarded.

To play an ongoing Story, you must have fewer than 3 ongoing Stories already in play.

Play it face-up. It remains until its own text ends it.

### Stratagems

If you have not played a new Stratagem this Battle, you may use your operation to play one from your hand face-up in your Stratagem area.

Pay its printed Command cost.

The Stratagem is active immediately and normally remains until Battle end.

## Maneuver precisely

To Maneuver:

1. choose one of your Named Formations;
2. pay 1 Command;
3. choose the adjacent Front to its left or right;
4. use the position in the same rank.

If that position is empty, move the Named Formation there.

If that position contains a friendly formation, exchange the full contents of the two positions.

Only the initiating formation must be Named.

A Maneuver cannot:

- move more than one Front;
- change rank;
- move onto the opponent's side;
- occur between Battles.

Unless a card says otherwise, Rear-to-Frontline or Frontline-to-Rear movement is not a Maneuver.

## What happens when attached cards leave?

During a Battle, when an effect removes or moves an attached card:

| What happens? | Result |
| --- | --- |
| A **Force** is discarded by an effect | Discard its Bond and Name with it. |
| A **Bond** is discarded by an effect | The Force stays. Return its Name to its owner's hand. |
| An open **Bond** is returned | Any Force stays. Put the Bond into its owner's hand. |
| A **Name** is returned or discarded | Any Force and Bond stay. The Bond becomes open. |
| A **Force** moves by a card effect | Its Bond and Name move with it unless the card says otherwise. |
| A **Named Formation Maneuvers** | Force, Bond, and Name move together. |
| A **Named Formation Retreats** | Force, Bond, and Name move together. |
| A **Rear Named Formation is driven off** | Discard Force, Bond, and Name together. |

**Battle cleanup is different:** incomplete formations are discarded, while complete Named Formations persist unless Retreat drives them off.

## Timing

There is no reaction stack.

For a normal card play:

1. announce the card and all required targets;
2. pay its Command cost;
3. resolve the played card completely;
4. apply active continuing effects.

For Maneuver:

1. announce the Named Formation and destination;
2. pay 1 Command;
3. move or exchange positions;
4. recalculate positional effects immediately.

When the first player Passes:

1. mark that player as Passed;
2. give the opponent exactly one final operation;
3. after that operation — or if the opponent Passes — end the Battle;
4. resolve all four Fronts.

At Battle end, card text that refers to a Front result, Retreat, Battle end, Story completion, or similar events resolves when that event occurs.

## Hidden and public information

**Hidden**

- cards in hand.

**Public**

- hand size;
- deck size;
- discard piles;
- all battlefield cards;
- all Stories;
- all Stratagems;
- each player's current Command;
- classifications and roles on visible cards;
- whether each player has played a new Hero or Stratagem this Battle.

## Hand limit and drawing

Your maximum hand size is **10**.

Whenever you would draw a card while already holding 10:

1. choose and discard 1 card from your hand;
2. draw the card.

This applies to the automatic start-of-turn draw and to card effects that make you draw.

Between Battles, simply draw until you reach 10.

## Deck construction

The canonical playtest decks contain exactly **34 cards** and currently use this composition:

- Exactly **14 Force-type cards**.
- Exactly **6 Names**.
- Heroes are **Unique**: maximum 1 copy of each Hero title.
- A deck may contain multiple different Heroes.
- Maximum 2 copies of a non-Unique title.
- Maximum 1 copy of any Unique title.
- Every printed Name is Unique.

Custom research decks may vary the Force and Name counts while retaining the deck size and copy limits.

## Glossary {#glossary}

**Battle**  
One period of alternating operations. After the first Pass, the opponent receives one final operation and then all four Fronts are resolved. A Battle has no overall winner.

**Bond**  
A formation component. It may be prepared before its Force. A Bond without a Name is an open Bond.

**Classification**  
A printed identity property such as *Human*, *God*, *King*, *Ship*, or *Stronghold*.

**Command**  
Your persistent war budget, tracked on a d20. Start at 20. Command spent on cards and Maneuver carries forward; some returns after each Battle.

**Command Collapse**  
The war-ending check after Command recovery. The current threshold is 5 Command.

**Deploy**  
Place a Force onto the battlefield. A printed deployment restriction applies only when the Force enters play.

**Driven off**  
What happens to a persistent Named Formation that loses its Front while already in the Rear. Discard the complete formation.

**Force**  
The card that activates a battlefield formation's Strength and Force-dependent rules.

**Front**  
One of the four battlefield columns.

**Hero**  
A Unique dual-use card that may be played as a Force or a Name. Each side may play one new Hero per Battle; persistent Heroes from previous Battles remain.

**Line Defense**  
The +1 Strength normally gained by a Force in the Frontline.

**Maneuver**  
A 1-Command operation that moves a Named Formation one adjacent Front left or right in the same rank, or exchanges it with a friendly formation there.

**Name**  
A Unique formation component. It may be prepared before its Force or Bond.

**Named Formation**  
A complete Force — Bond — Name. Named Formations persist between Battles unless removed or driven off.

**Open Bond**  
A Bond without a Name.

**Pass**  
End your operations in the current Battle. The opponent then receives exactly one final operation.

**Prepared**  
A Bond or Name placed in a formation before its Force. Force-dependent text stays inactive until the Force arrives.

**Rear**  
The rank behind the Frontline. A Named Formation that loses its Front while in Rear is driven off.

**Retreat**  
The Battle-end consequence for a Frontline Named Formation in a lost Front. It moves to Rear after any Rear Named Formation there is driven off.

**Role**  
A printed Force property such as *Swordsman*, *Spearman*, *Archer*, or *Healer*. Roles provide standard formation rules.

**Story**  
A face-up card representing an event, objective, or development. A Story may resolve immediately or remain ongoing according to its text. *Legend*, *Saga*, *Myth*, *Omen*, *Warning*, *Prophecy*, and *Conspiracy* are Story forms.

**Stratagem**  
A Battle-wide public card played face-up as your operation. Each player may play one new Stratagem per Battle.

**Strength**  
The value used to determine which player wins each Front.

*Unique*  
A printed property limiting that title to one copy per deck.

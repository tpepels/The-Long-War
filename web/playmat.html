THE LONG WAR · BATTLEFIELD OVERVIEW

Read the board before you read the rules.

You are not moving armies toward each other. You are building three simultaneous claims and deciding which two are worth winning.

1

Three Fronts

Left, Center, and Right are three separate contests. Winning one Front does nothing by itself; you normally need two.

2

Two positions per Front

Each player has a Frontline and Rear position. Together they make that player's Strength in the Front.

3

No attack step

Cards do not march across the line and fight. You add Strength, attach Links and Names, disrupt cards, and eventually choose to Pass.

4

Attachments stay with their Subject

A Link and Name attach to one Subject space. A heavily built Subject can dominate one Front, but it cannot win the other two for you.

5

Schemes create uncertainty

A face-down Scheme belongs to one Front. Your opponent knows where it is, but not what it will do.

6

Strength decides control

Add Frontline + Rear in each Front. Higher Strength controls that Front. Control two Fronts to win the Battle.

You win the Battle 2 Fronts to 1. The 11 Strength in Center is impressive, but you would have won Center with much less. That is why card economy matters.

THE LONG WAR · FIRST-GAME REFERENCE

What do I do now?

Keep this page beside the board. It is a sequence of decisions, not a miniature rulebook.

SETUP

Start with a hand, not a battlefield

Shuffle 30 cards. Draw 10. Mulligan up to 2. Randomly choose the first player.

YOUR TURN

Make one commitment

Play 1 card or Pass. Then the opponent acts.

No automatic draw. No attack phase.

PASS

You are done with this Battle

You cannot play again this Battle. Your opponent may continue alone until they also Pass.

Passing saves cards but gives up the chance to improve your board.
WHEN BOTH PASS

Score in this order

  1. Who controls 2 of 3 Fronts?
  2. If neither: who has more total Strength?
  3. If still tied: the first passer wins.
BETWEEN BATTLES

Reset the board, not your hand

Discard the battlefield. Keep your hand. Draw 3. The Battle loser chooses who starts next.
